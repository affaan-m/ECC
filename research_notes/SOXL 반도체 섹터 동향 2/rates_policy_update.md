## Export controls, tariffs, US-China

### Takeaway
The Nov 16 BIS remote-access rule is verified and is a license requirement (not a flat ban), keyed to a 12 million aggregate TPP data-center threshold, effective on Federal Register publication (expected Nov 19). Newly relevant: reported Commerce action on Megaspeed and affiliates on Nov 23, and a Senate hearing for Huang Dec 3. Section 232 phase two, APEC outcomes and rare earths remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
- BIS interim final rule "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", announced Nov 16, 2026: new license requirement for exports/re-exports/transfers of advanced computing items for data centers anywhere when there is knowledge the capacity serves entities headquartered in or owned by parents in Macau or Country Group D:5 (including China); covered data centers above 12 million aggregate TPP; existing covered data centers need a license within 90 days of the effective date; no license exceptions; rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule; extends the advanced-computing license requirement to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand (US-headquartered consignees excluded with monthly reporting); foundries/OSATs can no longer use transistor-count attestations for chips designed in listed countries — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/commerce-targets-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors-in-new-interim-final-rule), [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20261117-us-government-restricts-remote-access-to-advanced-ai-chips-and-formally-rescinds-the-ai-diffusion-rule), [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/bis-expands-advanced-computing-controls-to-address-data-center-remote-access-and-third-country-diversion.pdf)
- Effective on publication, expected Nov 19, 2026; comments due 60 days after publication (about Jan 18, 2027 per a search summary). The Morgan Lewis page says "announced but not yet published" as of Nov 18, so the Federal Register publication date is not independently confirmed — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/11/commerce-targets-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors-in-new-interim-final-rule)
- Scale example: about three GB300 NVL72 racks (about 4.3 million TPP each) or roughly 760 H100/H200 GPUs exceed the 12M TPP threshold (Morgan Lewis summary) — same source
- Context: tied to scrutiny of Singapore-based Megaspeed (suspected diversion of Nvidia chips); Boston Globe framed the rule as closing a loophole "before China trip" — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/11/16/nation/trump-administration-moves-close-ai-chip-loophole-before-china-trip/), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3374639/us-tightens-ai-chip-export-curbs-close-chinas-overseas-data-centre-loophole)
- HNGN reports Commerce added Megaspeed and six affiliates to its trade blacklist on Monday Nov 23 and Malaysia told data-center operators on Tuesday to stop serving the seven companies — [HNGN](https://www.hngn.com/articles/273950/20261124/dow-tops-50000-chip-stocks-slide-after-nvidia-rally.htm); conflicts with an earlier (undated, FT-sourced) report that Commerce delayed Entity List additions and left Megaspeed off — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/commerce-delays-entity-list-additions-megaspeed-stays-off). Likely sequential (delay, then action), but unconfirmed by a primary source.
- Malaysia's MITI said (Nov 13) it never cleared Megaspeed/Speedmatrix and is tightening re-export checks — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/11/13/miti-says-it-has-never-cleared-megaspeed-or-subsidiary-speedmatrix-tightens-checks-on-re-export-of-ai-chips)
