## Fed policy, Treasury yields, inflation and labor data, and effect on high-beta tech

### Takeaway
The Fed is in a tightening bias, not an easing one. It hiked 25bp on Sept 16, held at 3.75-4.00% on Oct 28 with two dissenters wanting a hike, and markets price a December 8-9 hike at roughly 78-80%+. The 10-year yield is about 5.10-5.15%, a multiyear high, which pressures long-duration, high-beta semis and a 3x leveraged ETF like SOXL.

### Cited Findings

The statement says "Inflation remains elevated", activity is expanding at a "strong pace", and uncertainty is elevated partly due to geopolitical developments — [Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)
- The Sept 16 meeting raised rates by 25bp, unanimously. Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation "has been running above the Committee's objective for close to 6 years" and that energy prices moved higher again. WTI was near $100, up about 78% YTD (per the article). CME FedWatch showed a 78% probability of a December hike. 10-year closed at 5.03% (intraday high 5.051%). - A hike path with the 10-year above 5% raises the discount rate on semis, which are up about 68% YTD per the Oct 13 article. That leaves large downside convexity for 3x leveraged products, especially around Dec 4, Dec 9 and Dec 10.
- A hot print or a 50bp hike (Waller's comment) is the main macro tail event before year-end. A softer print could produce a sharp relief rally, so both-way volatility favors decay in SOXL.

## US export controls on chips to China, tariffs and Section 232, CHIPS Act, US-China negotiations

### Takeaway
China revenue has effectively gone to zero at Nvidia: it said on its Nov 17 call that it is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data-center products to China, guidance excludes China, and new US rules on Nov 16 bar advanced chips in foreign data centers serving Chinese customers remotely. The US-China trade truce is being extended in short increments (China paused rare-earth curbs for two months on Nov 9) and export controls are flagged as a flashpoint ahead of a Trump-Xi meeting expected at APEC in Shenzhen.

### Cited Findings
- Nvidia sold $2.4bn of licensed Hopper-generation data-center products to China in the quarter ended Oct 25. Interim CEO Colette Kress said "we are no longer shipping licenced Hopper data centre products to China", and guidance includes none. The H200 pathway: January 2026 licensing eased to case-by-case review; US approved sales to 10 Chinese firms including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance; 25% tariff collected on chips routed through the US; Beijing has since restricted H200 imports; Reuters reported about 600,000 H200 chips stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment — [BDNews24/Reuters](https://bdnews24.com/business/pyentjtvr9)
- Rules issued Nov 16, 2026 bar advanced US chips from foreign data centers that serve Chinese customers remotely (per search summary of the same coverage; I did not locate the BIS rule text) — [BDNews24](https://bdnews24.com/business/pyentjtvr9)
- Section 232: a Jan 14, 2026 proclamation set a 25% tariff on a narrow set of advanced chips (effective Jan 15), as phase one. Phase two could bring "significant" broader tariffs with an offset program for US investment, depending on negotiations. Commerce/USTR reports were due Apr 14 and Jul 1, 2026. Framework deals with South Korea, Taiwan and Japan may lower exposure — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/president-trump-orders-narrowly-targeted-25-section-232-tariff-certain-advanced), [Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/help/onesource-global-trade/regulatory-insights/2026/january-15th/trump-admin-tariffs-advanced-ai-chips-232.html)
- US-China: Busan truce (Oct 2025) ran one year; China announced on Nov 9, 2026 a two-month suspension of some critical-mineral export controls, and the US is extending 178 Section 301 exclusions. USTR Greer stressed China must implement rare earth, ag commitments — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/11/09/china-extends-pause-on-rare-earth-export-curbs-for-two-months/)
- Atlantic Council (Nov 17, 2026) says the Nvidia scandal threatens to reignite tensions at Trump-Xi talks and export controls may be the major sticking point in 2027 (from search snippet; page not opened) — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/?p=961235)
- Nvidia CEO event: Jensen Huang resigned (reported around Nov 12-13) amid a report linking him to a Chinese businesswoman US authorities suspect of spying; Kress interim CEO; Nvidia -4.3% that day; stock at about 12x expected earnings, lowest since 2012, after an Oct 6 record high; Alibaba -7.1%; AMD +5.9%, Broadcom +5.5%. FT reported the board approached Arm CEO Rene Haas. Nvidia has had six straight weekly declines, longest since Oct 2012 — [NST/Reuters](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/corporate/2026/11/1576568/wall-street-ends-lower-nvidia-ceo-resigns-us-yields), [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/wall-street-nvidia-selloff-weekly-rebound-3809754)
- Conflicting signal: one search run returned no evidence of the resignation and described Huang as still CEO in older coverage; two independent outlets above (Reuters via NST, and BDNews24) confirm it, so treat the older material as stale.

### Inferences
- China is now a zero-weight in Nvidia guidance, so upside optionality exists only if Beijing and Washington both reopen; the Nov 16 remote-access rule narrows loopholes and is a headline risk for AMD, Broadcom and equipment names too.
- Leadership uncertainty at the largest SOXL constituent (Nvidia) adds idiosyncratic risk to a cap-weighted 3x product.

## Taiwan and geopolitical risk, rare earths and supply constraints, power and energy limits for AI datacenters

### Takeaway
Taiwan risk is elevated but not at crisis level (unannounced 100+ vessel Chinese deployment, no imminent blockade expected). Rare-earth leverage is on a rolling two-month pause. The physical AI build-out is gated by power equipment, with Bloomberg-sourced reporting that about half of planned 2026 US data-center builds are delayed or canceled.

### Cited Findings
- Early November 2026: China deployed over 100 naval and coast guard vessels around Taiwan and the western Pacific, unannounced; a Taiwanese official told Reuters the drills simulated attacks on opposing forces and cities. Defence minister Wellington Koo described area-denial drills with the Liaoning group. Analysts do not expect an imminent invasion or blockade; the activity precedes the APEC Shenzhen summit and Taiwan local elections at end of November — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/china-unannounced-naval-deployment-taiwan-blockade-3809720), [Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/11/13/could-china-blockade-taiwan-naval-drills-raise-fears-of-a-wider-indo-pacific-crisis/)
- Rare earths: China's suspension of its October 2025 curbs ran to Nov 10, 2026, and was extended two months from Nov 9 (to about early-mid January 2027, my arithmetic) — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/11/09/china-extends-pause-on-rare-earth-export-curbs-for-two-months/)
- Iran/oil: Trump said the US does not need a deal with Iran, pushing yields up; WTI near $100 per the Oct 28 article — [NST/Reuters](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/corporate/2026/11/1576568/wall-street-ends-lower-nvidia-ceo-resigns-us-yields), [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/news/fed-holds-rates-steady-oct-2026/)
- Power: GE Vernova gas-turbine backlog 100 GW in Q1 2026, at least 110 GW expected by year-end (some double-ordering). Large power transformer lead times 128-144 weeks in 2025 sources. Close to half of planned 2026 US data-center builds expected delayed or canceled despite more than $650bn planned 2026 AI spend; about 12 GW expected online in 2026, roughly a third under construction. PJM warns of transmission/reliability risk; developers turning to on-site gas (IEA, April 2026) — [Tom's Hardware/Bloomberg](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/half-of-planned-us-data-center-builds-have-been-delayed-or-canceled-growth-limited-by-shortages-of-power-infrastructure-and-parts-from-china-the-ai-build-out-flips-the-breakers), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/us-power-companies-scramble-data-centre-equipment)

### Inferences
- Power bottlenecks cap near-term GPU deployment timing, which can shift semis orders rightward even if demand is intact (supports bears' "air pocket" argument, though bulls read it as long-duration backlog).
- Taiwan and rare-earth risks are event-driven tails; the late-November Taiwan elections and APEC meeting are near-term catalysts.
